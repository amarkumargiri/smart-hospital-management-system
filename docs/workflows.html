Overview

Three roles, one episode

The platform has exactly three logins. The patient asks for care, the doctor delivers it, and reception runs everything else the hospital needs to make that possible.

Who does what

RoleOwnsCannot
Patient
PATIENT		 Their own bookings, records, reports and bills See any other patient; change clinical fields; see an unverified lab result
Doctor
DOCTOR		 Consultations, vitals, diagnoses, prescriptions, lab orders, admission requests, discharge summaries Open a chart with no treating relationship; allocate beds; touch stock or money
Reception
RECEPTIONIST		 Registration, scheduling, wards and ICU, admissions, emergency, theatres, laboratory, pharmacy, billing, analytics, forecasting, audit Diagnose or prescribe; complete a consultation; release lab results they entered themselves; reach another hospital
Why one desk role instead of seven A small hospital does not staff a separate lab technician, pharmacist, ward manager and billing clerk in software. Collapsing them into one reception role removes six permission matrices, six sets of navigation and six seed accounts, while the underlying workflows — sample collection, dispensing, bed allocation, payment — stay exactly as they were.

The outpatient episode, end to end

Patient Books a slot→ Reception Confirms→ Checks in, issues token→ Doctor Consults, diagnoses, prescribes→ Reception Lab, pharmacy, payment→ Follow-up

The inpatient branch

Doctor Requests admission→ Reception Approves, allocates bed→ Treatment, surgery→ Doctor Discharge summary→ Reception Final bill
The access rule behind every screen Access is role + hospital + relationship. A doctor sees a patient's chart only when an appointment, admission, lab order or prescription links them. Reception sees only patients with a record at its own hospital. Patients see only themselves. No account can read across hospitals — the platform-wide role does not exist. Every chart opened is written to the audit log with who, what and when.
Role 01

Patient

PATIENT

A person seeking care. Self-registers, finds a hospital and doctor, books, attends, and afterwards holds a permanent record of everything that happened.

Registration and first sign-in

  1. Create an account — name, email, mobile, password, date of birth, gender, blood group, address and emergency contact. Password must be at least 8 characters. POST /api/auth/register
  2. Verify with the OTP — a six-digit code activates the account. Until it is entered the account stays PENDING VERIFICATION and cannot be used. POST /api/auth/verify-otp
  3. Land on the dashboard — upcoming appointments, reports ready, active medication, outstanding balance, follow-ups due. GET /api/patients/{id}/dashboard

Finding care

  1. Search hospitals — filter by city, specialty, department, minimum rating, emergency availability, and "doctors available today". Each result shows live bed and ICU availability. GET /api/hospitals
  2. Open a hospital — departments, facilities, free beds, free ICU beds, free ventilators, and the full list of doctors. GET /api/hospitals/{id}
  3. Search doctors — by name or specialty, city, minimum experience, maximum fee, and availability on a chosen date. Only verified, active doctors appear. GET /api/doctors
  4. Read the doctor profile — qualification, experience, department, fee, languages, medical registration number, slot length and the published weekly clinic hours. GET /api/doctors/{id}

Booking an appointment

  1. Pick a date — the system expands that day's clinic windows into concrete slots, removes the ones already taken, and drops any slot already in the past. GET /api/doctors/{id}/slots?date=…
  2. Choose type and reason — OPD, follow-up, specialist consultation or teleconsultation, plus a short reason for the visit.
  3. Confirm the slot — the appointment is created as REQUESTED and both the patient and the doctor are notified. POST /api/appointments
  4. Reschedule or cancel — allowed while the appointment is requested or confirmed; cancelling asks for an optional reason and notifies the doctor. PUT /api/appointments/{id} · DELETE /api/appointments/{id}
Two people, one slot Slots are protected by a uniqueness rule on doctor and time. If someone else takes the slot in the seconds between it being shown and being confirmed, the booking is rejected with "This time slot is no longer available. Please select another slot." and the list refreshes.

Appointment lifecycle as the patient sees it

REQUESTED→ CONFIRMED→ CHECKED IN→ IN CONSULTATION→ COMPLETED or CANCELLED REJECTED NO-SHOW

On the day

  1. Check in at reception — a token is issued at check-in, not at booking, so the queue reflects who is actually present.
  2. Watch the queue — own token, token now being served, patients ahead, and an estimated wait based on how long today's consultations with that doctor have actually taken. GET /api/appointments/queue/status
  3. Consultation — the doctor records symptoms, vitals, notes, diagnosis, prescription and any lab tests. Nothing is released to the patient until the consultation is completed.

Afterwards

ScreenWhat the patient gets
Medical recordsHealth profile, every consultation with diagnosis, notes and vitals, and every hospital admission with its discharge summary
PrescriptionsEach prescription with medicine, dose, frequency, duration, route, instructions and how much has been dispensed. Printable to PDF
Lab reportsResults with units, reference ranges and flags. A report that is not yet verified is not shown as a result
Bills & paymentsItemised bills, outstanding amount, and payment by UPI, card or net banking
ProfilePersonal details, insurance policies, two-factor toggle, and a list of active sessions that can be revoked individually
NotificationsBooking confirmations, report ready, prescription issued, payment received, follow-up due, discharge
What a patient can never do
  • Open another patient's record — the API answers "You do not have permission to access this record."
  • Edit allergies or chronic conditions — those are written by clinical staff only.
  • Move an appointment to any status other than cancelled.
  • See a lab result before the laboratory has verified it.
  • Pay more than the outstanding balance on a bill.
Role 02

Doctor

DOCTOR

Registers against a hospital, waits for reception to verify them, then runs a clinic: accepting appointments, consulting, diagnosing, prescribing, ordering tests, admitting and discharging.

Getting on the platform

  1. Self-register — name, contact, hospital, department, medical registration number, specialization, qualification, experience, consultation fee and slot length. POST /api/doctors/register
  2. Wait for verification — the account is created PENDING VERIFICATION. An unverified doctor does not appear in patient search and cannot receive appointments. Reception at their hospital activates them.
  3. Publish clinic hours — windows per weekday, for example Monday 09:00–13:00 and 17:00–20:00. The whole week is replaced in one transaction so the schedule is never left half-updated. Patients can only book inside these windows, on exact slot boundaries. PUT /api/doctors/{id}/availability

A clinic day

  1. Open the dashboard — today's list, pending requests awaiting approval, follow-ups due, new lab results with critical ones counted separately, and current inpatients. GET /api/doctors/me/dashboard
  2. Accept or decline requests — accepting moves the appointment to confirmed and notifies the patient. POST /api/appointments/{id}/status
  3. Start the consultation — opening the consultation screen creates the consultation and moves the appointment to IN CONSULTATION in one step. Re-opening it resumes rather than duplicating. POST /api/consultations

Inside the consultation

Left: the record being written

  • Symptoms, clinical notes, treatment plan, follow-up date
  • Vitals — temperature, blood pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate, SpO₂, weight, height
  • Diagnoses with ICD-10 code, severity and a primary flag
  • Prescription lines — medicine, dose, frequency, duration, route, instruction
  • Lab tests selected from the hospital's catalogue

Right: the history that informs it

  • Blood group, allergies and chronic conditions shown as warnings
  • Previous consultations with diagnosis and notes
  • Previous prescriptions
  • Previous lab orders and results
  • Past admissions and discharge summaries
Completing the consultation does five things at once
  • Marks the consultation complete and closes the appointment.
  • Writes a medical-record entry titled with the primary diagnosis.
  • Raises the consultation bill from the doctor's fee.
  • Notifies the patient that notes and prescription are available.
  • If a follow-up date was set, schedules the reminder.
It is refused if no diagnosis has been recorded.

Beyond the clinic

ActionWhat happens
Order lab testsRoutine, urgent or STAT. The reception lab queue sorts STAT first, then oldest within each band
Review resultsVerified results arrive as notifications; critical flags are marked CRITICAL
Request admissionCreates a request with reason and working diagnosis. Refused if the patient already has an open admission
Schedule surgeryBooks a theatre; overlapping bookings in the same theatre are rejected
DischargeWrites the discharge summary — diagnosis, procedures, medication, investigations, instructions, follow-up date — frees the bed to cleaning, and generates the consolidated final bill
Cancel a prescriptionOnly the prescribing doctor, and only while nothing has been dispensed against it
Boundaries
  • A chart opens only where a treating relationship exists — appointment, admission, lab order or prescription.
  • Another doctor's consultation cannot be edited or completed.
  • Beds are requested, never allocated directly; reception owns bed state.
  • No access to inventory, billing, analytics or the forecasting dashboard.
  • Every chart view and every record change is written to the audit log.
Role 03

Reception

RECEPTIONIST

The hospital desk. Everything that is not a clinical judgement runs from here: the front desk, the wards, the laboratory, the pharmacy, the cash counter and the planning dashboards.

Front desk

  1. Open the appointments board — filtered by date range, doctor, status or patient name. Defaults to today through the coming week. GET /api/appointments
  2. Book for a patient — search by name, mobile or email; pick the doctor and date; the dialog offers only the slots the API says are genuinely free. POST /api/appointments
  3. Confirm and check in — check-in issues the token (A-01, A-02, …) numbered per doctor per day, and places the patient in the queue. POST /api/appointments/{id}/status
  4. Record vitals — against an open consultation, before the doctor sees the patient. Ranges are validated — temperature 25–45 °C, systolic 40–300, SpO₂ 30–100 — so a mistyped figure is rejected rather than stored. PUT /api/consultations/{id}/vitals
  5. Register emergency arrivals — walk-ins can be registered with a name and phone alone — no account required — with a triage level and presenting complaint. The queue sorts critical first, then by arrival time. POST /api/emergency

Wards, ICU and theatres

AVAILABLE→ OCCUPIED→ CLEANING→ AVAILABLE also RESERVED MAINTENANCE
  1. Read the ward map — general, ICU, isolation, private and emergency beds by ward, colour-coded by status, with occupancy percentages, free ventilators and critical patients in ICU. GET /api/beds · GET /api/beds/summary
  2. Create capacity — beds are created in bulk: a prefix and a number range builds a whole ward at once, skipping numbers that already exist. Hospital bed and ICU capacity update themselves from the bed table, so the public profile and the forecast denominators are never typed twice. POST /api/beds/bulk
  3. Approve admissions and allocate beds — the picker offers only free beds. Claiming the bed and admitting the patient happen in one transaction, so two patients can never land in the same bed. Transfers claim the new bed and release the old one together. POST /api/admissions/{id}/admit · /transfer
  4. Run the theatre board — schedule surgeries with theatre, surgeon, procedure, time, duration and charge. The theatre status follows the surgery: in use while operating, cleaning afterwards. POST /api/ot/surgeries
The race that matters Bed allocation uses a conditional update: the bed is claimed only if it is still available at that instant. If it was taken a moment earlier, the response is "That bed is no longer available. Please choose another bed." and the picker refreshes. An occupied bed also cannot be edited directly — "Discharge the patient before changing this bed status."

Laboratory

ORDERED→ SAMPLE COLLECTED→ PROCESSING→ RESULT ENTERED→ VERIFIED or CANCELLED
  1. Work the queue — orders sorted STAT first, then urgent, then routine, oldest first inside each band. GET /api/lab/orders
  2. Collect and process — assign the sample identifier; collection time and collector are stamped automatically. POST /api/lab/orders/{id}/collect · /process
  3. Enter results — value, unit, reference range and a flag per test. A result referring to a test not on the order is rejected. The order moves as a whole, so a half-filled report never reaches the patient. POST /api/lab/orders/{id}/results
  4. Have a colleague release it — verification makes the report visible to the patient, writes a medical-record entry, and notifies the patient and the ordering doctor — flagged critical if any value is. POST /api/lab/orders/{id}/verify
Four-eyes rule survives the simplification The account that entered the results cannot verify them: "Results must be verified by someone other than the technician who entered them." This is why the hospital needs at least two reception accounts, and why the seed creates two.

Pharmacy

  1. Check the three alert counts — low stock, expiring within thirty days, already expired. GET /api/pharmacy/alerts
  2. Receive stock — medicine, form, strength, batch, quantity, reorder level, expiry, supplier and price. Re-adding an existing medicine and batch tops up that batch rather than creating a duplicate row. POST /api/pharmacy/inventory
  3. Dispense — match each prescribed line to a batch in stock. Stock is decremented, the dispensed quantity is recorded against the prescription, and a dispense record is written — all in one transaction. Anything that crosses its reorder level raises an immediate notification. POST /api/pharmacy/dispense
Refused at the counter
  • Dispensing from an expired batch — "… has expired."
  • Dispensing more than the batch holds — "Only N unit(s) of … are in stock."
  • Dispensing against a cancelled prescription, or one from another hospital.
  • Adding stock that is already past its expiry date.

Billing

TriggerBill produced
Doctor completes a consultationConsultation fee. Idempotent — a retry returns the same bill, never a duplicate
Patient is dischargedConsolidated stay: room or ICU nights (part-days round up, minimum one), verified lab and imaging, completed surgeries, medicines dispensed during the stay
Reception creates oneManual bill for walk-in services, with any line items

Line items can be added or removed across nine categories, with a discount or tax; every change recalculates the bill from its items, so the total can never disagree with what it is made of. Payments are taken by UPI, card, net banking, cash or insurance, and a settled insurance claim automatically writes a payment for the approved amount.

Guards
  • No line items may be added to a bill that is paid, cancelled or refunded.
  • A payment larger than the outstanding amount is refused — "Payment exceeds the outstanding amount of N."
  • A claim cannot exceed the bill total, and the policy must belong to that patient.

Forecasting

The planning dashboard, and the feature that makes this more than a booking system.

  1. Capture the day — a daily snapshot rolls live activity into one row per hospital per day: OPD, admissions, discharges, occupied beds and ICU beds, theatre cases, oxygen, blood by group and revenue. POST /api/forecast/data/snapshot
  2. Check data quality first — usable days, date span, and issues grouped by rule. Rows with negative quantities, duplicate dates, future dates or occupancy above capacity are excluded from training; outliers are flagged but kept, because a genuine surge is what the model needs to learn. GET /api/forecast/data/quality
  3. Run the forecast — seven metrics across three horizons plus eight blood groups — 45 forecasts, generated in about 150 ms over two years of data. POST /api/forecast/run
  4. Read the dashboard — per metric: current value, forecast average, percentage change, the winning model and its MAPE. The chart shows recent actuals, the prediction, and a 95% confidence band. GET /api/forecast/dashboard
  5. Act on alerts and acknowledge them — each carries a recommended action. POST /api/forecast/alerts/{id}/acknowledge

How a model is chosen Four models — moving average, seasonal naive, linear trend and Holt-Winters with a seven-day season — each predict a held-out window of recent history. The one with the lowest MAPE wins and is refit on the full series before producing the real forecast. Different metrics pick different winners, and the dashboard names the winner and its error rate beside every number, so a forecast is never presented as more certain than it is.
